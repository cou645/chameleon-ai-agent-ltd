Security & vulnerability disclosure

How to report a security problem in the Chameleon AI website or the Chameleon Accounting software, and what we do about it.

📇 security.txt (RFC 9116) 🖊️ Signed releases (Ed25519)

Published: 2 September 2026  ·  Last reviewed: 5 September 2026

1. Reporting a vulnerability

Email security@chameleon-ai-agent-ltd.net. Please do not post security problems in a public forum or a support ticket before we have had a chance to fix them.

Tell us:

  • what the issue is and where — the website page, or the software component / version (chameleon_accounting.py --version);
  • how to reproduce it: steps, a proof of concept, or a short script;
  • what impact you think it has.

If you need to send us something sensitive and want it encrypted, ask in a first email and we will arrange a secure channel.

2. What you can expect from us

  • an acknowledgement within 3 working days;
  • an assessment, a severity, and a target fix date, with progress updates;
  • coordinated disclosure: we aim to release a fix within 90 days and will credit you by name in the release notes if you would like that;
  • there is no paid bug bounty — we recognise reporters in the release notes.

3. Safe harbour

We will not pursue or support legal action against anyone who tests in good faith and within this policy. That means: only against your own installation and your own test data; without accessing, altering or deleting anyone else's data; without degrading the service for others; and without social engineering or physical attacks. If you come across personal data, stop and tell us.

4. Out of scope

  • Third-party services the software connects to — HMRC, GoCardless, TrueLayer, Companies House. Report those to the provider.
  • Volumetric denial-of-service testing.
  • Automated-scanner output with no demonstrated impact.
  • Missing hardening headers on the marketing site with no working exploit.

5. How the software is protected

  • Data stays on your device. The ledger, the HMRC OAuth tokens and your API credentials are stored locally and are never routed through us.
  • Signed updates. The release manifest and download archives are signed with an Ed25519 key. The software verifies the signature offline against a built-in public key and ignores anything that does not verify. You choose when to upgrade; nothing is installed automatically. The public key is at /.well-known/chameleon-accounting-release.pub.
  • Local hardening check. chameleon_accounting.py --security-check audits file permissions, token age, the TLS trust store and the OAuth configuration on the machine; --security-check --fix tightens what it can.
  • Standard TLS. Connections use the operating system's certificate store. We do not pin certificates.

6. AI features and your data

AI features are optional, bring-your-own-key, and send data from your device directly to the AI provider you choose (Terms & Conditions §9). We are sometimes asked why we don't instead disguise the figures mathematically before sending them — for example doubling every amount and halving the AI's answer — so nothing real ever leaves the device. We looked at this and it doesn't hold up:

  • A fixed scaling factor preserves every ratio in the data. UK VAT is 20% of net sales; double both figures and the 20% relationship still holds exactly, so the shape of the data — and often the scaling factor itself — is recoverable from the request alone. It is obfuscation, not encryption, with no real security margin.
  • It does nothing for the parts of a document that are text, not numbers — supplier names, descriptions, references — which are usually the more sensitive part.
  • It undermines the reason to use AI at all: a model's judgement about what's "a large transaction" or unusual for a business is calibrated on real-world magnitudes, so scaling either breaks that judgement or is silently reversed by the model inferring the true scale from context.
  • Computing on genuinely encrypted data without decrypting it is a real field (homomorphic encryption / secure multiparty computation) — but it does not exist today for "send encrypted figures to a commercial AI model and get a coherent answer". We won't advertise a protection we can't actually provide.

So we say plainly instead: if you use an AI feature, your data is sent to and may be retained by the provider you chose, under their policy. The only architecture that is genuinely zero-transfer is a model that runs entirely on your own device, which is on our roadmap as an alternative, not a replacement, for bring-your-own-key.

7. If we have a security incident

If we become aware of a breach affecting personal or customer data, or the security of the Making Tax Digital service or HMRC credentials, then within 72 hours of becoming aware we will:

  1. contain it — isolate the affected system, preserve evidence, revoke exposed credentials;
  2. notify HMRC by logging a ticket at developer.service.hmrc.gov.uk/devhub-support/tickets, with a named breach contact and a direct telephone number;
  3. notify the Information Commissioner's Office where the breach involves personal data and meets the risk threshold, via ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach;
  4. notify affected customers without undue delay where the risk to them is high, explaining what happened and what to do;
  5. publish a fix and a release note.

See our Privacy Policy for how we handle personal data.
